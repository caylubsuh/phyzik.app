# Marketing — Press & PR Strategy

**Owner:** Caleb.
**Budget estimate:** $0–5k (for a targeted PR freelancer if we want help).
**Grounded in:** `research/growth-case-studies.md` §Hevy §Whoop §Cal AI, existing `app/press/page.tsx` (live, ready).

PR is a slow lever — weeks to months to move. But it compounds. One good TechCrunch piece becomes Wikipedia-citeable. One good Men's Health piece becomes a partnership email. A piece in a niche publication (Barbend, T-Nation) delivers the most aligned traffic per visitor of any channel we have.

This doc is the 6-week PR sprint plan, acknowledging that the main ROI arrives in month 2–3.

---

## 1. Realistic expectations

### 1.1 What PR can deliver in 6 weeks

- **Niche fitness press coverage** (Barbend, Generation Iron, BoxRox for Hyrox, Muscle & Fitness, T-Nation). Achievable in 2–4 weeks of outreach.
- **Local tech/lifestyle coverage** (your local tech scene, if applicable). Achievable.
- **1 Product Hunt launch.** High-effort day-of, manageable scope. Achievable.
- **1 tier-2 tech outlet** (The Verge's small piece, 9to5Mac, TechCrunch if founder intro). Aspirational.

### 1.2 What PR can NOT deliver in 6 weeks

- **A tier-1 tech outlet feature** (full TechCrunch piece, NYT, WSJ). Without an existing relationship or pre-existing traction. Don't bank on this.
- **Major mainstream lifestyle coverage** (Men's Health cover story, Women's Health feature). These have 2–3 month lead times and require a news hook.
- **Viral moment.** PR doesn't manufacture virality; it captures it after the fact.

### 1.3 What PR IS actually for, at this stage

Three real jobs:
1. **Credibility seeding.** A press page with real logos on it raises conversion on influencer outreach, ambassador pitches, and even App Store browsing trust.
2. **SEO backlinks.** Quality inbound links from established domains rank the site faster for "workout app," "hyrox training app," etc.
3. **Journalist relationships.** The journalist you pitch in week 2 becomes the one who covers your Series A in 2027. Sow seeds.

**What PR is NOT for, at this stage:**
- Direct install conversion. A TechCrunch headline drives maybe 500 installs, then fades. Paid influencer at same cost drives more.
- Fundraising signal. Investors don't care that we got covered by Men's Health.

## 2. The three-track PR plan

### Track 1 — Niche fitness press (primary)

The highest-conversion readers live here. Fewer readers per piece, higher intent per reader.

**Target outlets:**
- **Barbend** (barbend.com) — powerlifting + strength sports. Their readers are PHYZIK's core demographic. Pitch: "New training app with evidence-based programming for strength athletes launches."
- **BoxRox** (boxrox.com) — CrossFit + Hyrox coverage. Pitch: "PHYZIK adds dedicated Hyrox programming with Stockholm-bound pro athlete on staff."
- **Generation Iron** (generationiron.com) — bodybuilding-focused. Pitch: "PHYZIK's Head of Bodybuilding [Sophia] brings RP-style programming to new social training app." Get quotes from Sophia.
- **Muscle & Fitness** (muscleandfitness.com) — broader bodybuilding consumer. Harder to land; pitch if Sophia has existing relationships.
- **T-Nation** (t-nation.com) — training methodology. Pitch: "App brings volume landmarks + mesocycle structure to mainstream lifters." Methodology-focused.
- **Breaking Muscle** (breakingmuscle.com) — programming + functional.
- **Outside Magazine** (outsideonline.com) — hybrid athletics, Hyrox, adventure fitness. Pitch: "How PHYZIK's Hyrox programming supports hybrid athletes training for the world's fastest-growing race." Tenkara angle.

**Lead time:** 1–3 weeks from first contact to published piece. Fastest movers.

**Outreach strategy:** Find the specific journalist who covers programming/new-apps (not the general editor). Email with a clear news hook + unique angle + 2-line ask. See §4.

### Track 2 — Tech/launch press

Establishes us as a legitimate product with a real company behind it.

**Target outlets:**
- **Product Hunt** — day-of launch coordinated. Aim for top 5 of the day. Requires pre-launch hunter relationship, hunters lined up, email list primed.
- **9to5Mac** — iOS apps are their beat. Pitch launch + notable feature (Apple Health integration, widgets, Live Activities).
- **The Verge** — if we have a compelling narrative (founder story, notable investor, unusual traction). Caleb's story (student founder, social-first approach) is pitchable.
- **TechCrunch** — unrealistic without intro, but worth one pitch. Ask any investor or advisor for warm intro.
- **MacStories** — iOS-focused, respected review site. Great writeup is worth a lot.
- **Fast Company** — if we have a design angle (the community page is pitchable as "how a fitness startup is avoiding the stock-photo fitness trap").

**Lead time:** 2–6 weeks for scheduled pieces; Product Hunt is day-of.

**Outreach strategy:** Reserve best energy for Product Hunt launch week. Pitch 2–3 tech outlets in that window with a clear news hook ("PHYZIK launches on Product Hunt today, designed to replace 4 apps with 1").

### Track 3 — Niche + local + founder-story

Long tail. Lower effort per pitch, varied returns.

**Targets:**
- Your local tech/startup publication (if applicable).
- Podcast invites — Mind Pump, 3D Muscle Journey, Barbell Shrugged, Lift Your Legacy. Founders are podcastable; reach + relationship compounds.
- University/college press if Caleb has a school angle.
- Strength-sport federation newsletters (USAPL, Hyrox).

**Lead time:** Variable. Some fast, some forever.

## 3. News hooks — what we actually pitch

Journalists need a *story*, not an announcement. "Our app launched" isn't a story. Here are pitchable angles:

### 3.1 The primary story: "The training app for people who take training seriously"

**Hook:** Training apps have split into two camps — sterile loggers (Hevy, Strong) and gamified retention traps (Fitbod, the peloton ecosystem). PHYZIK is aiming for a third thing: serious programming, social accountability, no gimmicks. Built by a student team shipping on Apple platforms.

**Who cares:** Fitness media, tech media interested in "anti-gamification" narratives, bodybuilding press.

**Assets:** Founder interview, team story, side-by-side product comparison.

### 3.2 "Hypertrophy science, pocketsize"

**Hook:** MEV / MAV / MRV were gym nerd jargon five years ago. Now they're mainstream. PHYZIK productizes the full Renaissance Periodization playbook — without the subscription cost of RP Hypertrophy, with social accountability Hevy can't match.

**Who cares:** Barbend, T-Nation, Breaking Muscle, Generation Iron.

**Assets:** Sophia-authored thought piece or Q&A, screenshots of volume tracking, quotes from the methodology section of the app.

### 3.3 "Hyrox in one app"

**Hook:** Hyrox is the fastest-growing fitness race format. Its athletes currently juggle 3+ apps (strength log, running tracker, programming PDF). PHYZIK unifies the stack. Pro athlete Tenkara trains on it.

**Who cares:** BoxRox, Outside, CrossFit-affiliated press, Hyrox-specific media (Hyrox itself has a newsletter).

**Assets:** Tenkara interview, training block walk-through, race-prep content.

### 3.4 "Inclusive programming without the pink dumbbells"

**Hook:** Women's training apps default to a sanitized aesthetic. PHYZIK doesn't build a separate women's experience — it builds one app, for every lifter, and means it. Sophia + Hannah on the team.

**Who cares:** Women's Health, Shape, fitness-angled lifestyle press, Stylist.

**Assets:** /for/women landing page, interviews with Sophia, Hannah, athlete testimonials. Honest position statement from Caleb.

### 3.5 "Design-led fitness tech"

**Hook:** Fitness apps look like they were built in 2018. PHYZIK's marketing site and app design challenge that. Why design matters in a category dominated by stock photography.

**Who cares:** Fast Company, design-focused outlets, Dezeen.

**Assets:** Site screenshots, app screenshots, design philosophy writeup.

### 3.6 "Founder story"

**Hook:** Student-founder team building for a demanding niche. Lean, fast, shipping at a speed larger competitors can't match.

**Who cares:** Local tech press, student-entrepreneur publications, podcast circuit.

**Assets:** Caleb + Vijay bio, path to founding, candid conversation about being a student shipping a fitness app.

## 4. The pitch email template

**Rules:**
- 150 words max.
- Subject line is a single newsworthy phrase, not a question.
- First line states the news or angle. Not "Dear X, my name is..."
- One specific hook tailored to their beat.
- One ask: "Interested in covering?" or "Happy to send full release / do a call."
- Signature: name, role, phone (journalists will call on a deadline).

**Example — Barbend pitch:**

Subject: `New training app brings MEV/MAV/MRV programming to lifters — launching this week`

> Hi [Journalist Name],
>
> Barbend's [recent piece on volume landmarks] is the kind of coverage we'd hope to complement. I'm launching PHYZIK this week — a training app that productizes the volume-landmark methodology Renaissance Periodization readers already know, with social accountability no logger currently offers.
>
> The app: evidence-based mesocycle design, auto-progressive overload, squad-based accountability. Our Head of Bodybuilding (Sophia [last name]) has competitive prep experience and leads the programming voice.
>
> Two possible angles:
> 1. **Product review**: Happy to give you beta access + a detailed walk-through.
> 2. **Methodology Q&A**: Sophia can do a focused piece on applying volume landmarks to natural lifters.
>
> Attached a 1-page brief. Five minutes on a call this week if interested.
>
> Best,
> Caleb Suh
> Founder, PHYZIK
> caleb@phyzik.app · [phone]

### 4.1 Attachment

One-page PDF, NOT a deck:
- Product screenshot (the best one).
- 3 bullets on what it does.
- 2 bullets on what makes it different.
- 1 quote from Caleb or Sophia.
- Contact info.

## 5. Product Hunt launch

### 5.1 Pre-launch (weeks 1–3)

- **Pick the date.** Tuesday-Thursday only (weekend launches die). Avoid major tech-news days.
- **Find a hunter.** A top-100 hunter dramatically lifts visibility. Reach out 3–4 weeks ahead. Good candidates: follow-up with Ryan Hoover if alumni network applies; lookup top-hunters in fitness/productivity space.
- **Build the asset pack:**
  - 1080x1080 thumbnail (app icon + name).
  - 2560x1600 gallery images (3–5 polished screenshots).
  - 60-second demo video.
  - Tagline (< 60 chars, not generic).
  - First comment from Caleb: honest story + 2–3 links (app store, site, Twitter).
- **Build the launch day email list.** Every friend, every ambassador, every tester, every Twitter follower. Email at 6am PT day-of: "PHYZIK is live on Product Hunt — would mean a lot to upvote if you dig it. [link]."
- **Line up 10–15 early comments** from the ambassador roster. Organic, not scripted. Real experience feedback, not "this is the best thing ever" spam.

### 5.2 Launch day

- Post 00:01 PT. Confirm via hunter or direct.
- First comment at 00:05 with the founder story + direct Caleb phone/email for feedback.
- Caleb monitors comments, replies to every single one within 30 minutes for the first 6 hours.
- Don't buy upvotes, don't ask for upvotes in exchange for anything. Product Hunt bans the app, not just the post.

### 5.3 Post-launch

- Top 5 of the day is a reasonable target.
- Whatever happens: write a public post-mortem (honest numbers, what worked, what didn't) 1 week later. PH community respects it; it extends the lifespan of the moment.

## 9. Measurement

### 9.1 What to track

- **Outreach:** Journalists contacted / responded / agreed to cover (funnel).
- **Coverage:** Pieces published, by outlet, with backlinks to phyzik.app.
- **Traffic from coverage:** Referral from each press URL in analytics.
- **Domain authority lift:** Monitor via Ahrefs or similar once a month.
- **SEO ranking lift** for target terms ("hypertrophy app," "hyrox training app," etc.).

### 9.2 What good looks like (6-week baseline)

- 3–5 niche fitness pieces published.
- 1–2 tier-2 tech pieces (Product Hunt + 9to5Mac or MacStories).
- 1–2 podcast appearances.
- Inbound domain authority + 2–5 points (small).
- Long-tail SEO ranking improvements on niche queries.

### 9.3 What great looks like

- 1 tier-1 tech feature (unlikely but possible).
- 1 Men's Health or equivalent (with 6-8 week lead time already seeded).
- 5+ podcast appearances.

## 10. Do we hire a PR freelancer?

**Arguments for:**
- Time cost. Caleb spending 15 hours/week on PR outreach has huge opportunity cost.
- Network. A good freelancer has pre-existing journalist relationships.
- Craft. Writing good pitches is a skill; most founders are amateurish at it.

**Arguments against:**
- Cost ($3–8k/month for a decent fitness PR freelancer).
- Journalists smell agency-pitches and reply less.
- Early-stage founder-pitches often land better because they feel genuine.

**Recommendation:** Caleb runs PR solo for weeks 1–2. If the funnel is clogged (<20% response rate on warm-targeted pitches), hire a freelancer for months 2–3 on a 90-day contract with specific deliverables.

Good freelancer candidates: find via the masthead of recent fitness-tech stories. See who's quoted as "comms for [Company]." Cold email them.

## 11. What we don't do

1. **Paid press releases** (PR Newswire, Business Wire). They don't drive coverage; they're SEO noise.
2. **"Guaranteed placement" services.** They pay blog networks to run content; Google discounts these and they hurt domain authority.
3. **Mass-email blasts to 200 journalists.** Every single one detects the template. Kills your domain reputation. Surgical targeting only.
4. **Paying journalists or outlets for coverage.** Disclosed or not, it's a career-ending move for both parties when discovered. Never.
5. **Announcing before we can deliver.** If we pitch Product Hunt for week 4 and the app isn't ready, the launch moment is wasted. Only pitch when shipping is imminent.
