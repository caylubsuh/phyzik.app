# Marketing — Influencer Tiers & Playbook

**Owner:** Caleb (lead), Hannah (content coordination, Tier 3–4).
**Budget estimate:** $5k–40k first 90 days depending on aggression.
**Grounded in:** `research/growth-case-studies.md` §Cal AI §Whoop §MyFitnessPal §Duolingo, `research/competitors.md` §Strong §Hevy.

Influencers are **transactional**: we pay them (cash, product, or equity) in exchange for specific deliverables on a specific timeline. Unlike ambassadors (see `ambassador-program.md`), influencer relationships are campaign-based. Overlap exists — a Tier 1 influencer can become a Founding Athlete over time — but plan them separately.

---

## 1. The philosophical stance

### 1.1 Why influencer spend can (and often does) fail

- **High CPM, low intent.** $10k to a fitness influencer can deliver 500k impressions and 50 installs. That's a $200 CAC on a free app — broken.
- **Audience mismatch.** Most fitness influencers' audiences are aesthetically-motivated, not training-seriously motivated. They want a body, not a program.
- **One-shot posts disappear.** Instagram kills post reach at day 3. $5k for a post that lives 72 hours is a bad bet unless it converts in those 72 hours.
- **The inflection problem.** Influencer spend scales linearly. Referral scales exponentially. Dollar for dollar, referral wins after the first month.

### 1.2 Why we still do it

- **Trust seeding.** A credible voice endorsing the app shortens the trust cycle for their followers.
- **Content gravity.** One good influencer post becomes the creative asset used in paid ads for 6 months.
- **Niche penetration.** Micro-influencers in narrow niches (raw powerlifting, Hyrox, specific federations) deliver higher conversion than broad fitness.
- **Press coattails.** Influencer mentions become PR hooks. Journalists reading "Sophia Smith endorses PHYZIK" use it as a quote without further verification.

### 1.3 The rule

**Spend influencer dollars only on campaigns with measurable delta.** If we can't attribute installs to a specific influencer post (via custom code, link, or referral badge), we can't learn from it. Every dollar spent should generate a data point about CAC, conversion, or retention of that cohort.

## 2. The tier structure

### Tier 1 — Mega (1M+ followers)

**Examples we would NOT chase in the first 90 days:**
- Jeff Nippard (4M+) — not available at our budget.
- Joe DeLaney (too big for our fit).
- Chris Bumstead / Nick Walker tier.

**Why skip:** CPM is $20k–100k per post. A single Reel costs more than our 90-day influencer budget. ROI for an early-stage app is almost never positive here. Revisit at 100k users.

**The exception:** If a mega-influencer is personally a PHYZIK user and wants to post organically, accept it — don't pay for it. Cal AI had this happen with a 5M-follower account and it was the single biggest moment in their growth (`growth-case-studies.md` §Cal AI). Engineer for the possibility; don't count on it.

### Tier 2 — Macro (100k–1M followers)

**Examples in our target audience:**
- Stephanie Buttermore (but too big / too aesthetics-focused; skip).
- Powerlifting champions with 200k–500k followings who do coaching content.
- Hyrox pros with 100k+ (Lucy Davis-adjacent).
- Hypertrophy YouTubers with 200k–800k subscribers (the Renaissance Periodization orbit).

**Typical deal structure:**
- 1 IG post + 3 Stories + optional Reel: $5k–15k per post.
- 1 YouTube integration (60-second mid-roll in a lifting video): $3k–8k.
- Usage rights for 6 months on their content: +$2k–5k.

**Target count:** 2–4 Tier 2 campaigns in the 6-week window.

**Realistic install volume per Tier 2 post:**
- IG post with medium audience fit: 80–300 installs.
- YouTube integration with high audience fit: 200–600 installs.
- Reel with viral potential: 500–2000 installs (rare; budget for the mean not the tail).

**CAC at this tier:** $20–100 for aligned audiences; $100+ for misaligned. We won't know until we run 2 and measure.

### Tier 3 — Micro (10k–100k followers)

**This is the highest-ROI tier.** Per `growth-case-studies.md` §Cal AI, most of their growth came from micro-influencers stacked (dozens of them), not megas.

**Target archetypes:**
- Competitive amateur lifters with growing followings.
- Strength coaches at recognized gyms (Hybrid Performance Method alumni, Cressey Sports Performance network, etc.).
- Hyrox regional competitors.
- Bodybuilding prep coaches with an engaged following.
- Women's lifting voices who are trusted in the Pvolve / Bret Contreras orbit.

**Typical deal structure:**
- Merch + 3-month free Pro + $300–1500 per post.
- Alternative (preferred for many): gifting + custom referral link with rev share on Pro upgrades.
- No required caption language. We give bullet points; they write.

**Target count:** 20–40 Tier 3 activations in 6 weeks. This is the volume play.

**Install volume per Tier 3 post:** 20–150 typically. Low individual, meaningful cumulative.

**CAC:** $5–30 per install at good fit. The winning tier.

### Tier 4 — Nano (<10k followers, niche authority)

**Target archetypes:**
- High school / college strength coaches with small but devoted audiences.
- Gym owners with 1k–10k local followings.
- Specific-niche voices: Masters-division powerlifters, adaptive lifting, specific-injury comeback accounts.
- College athletes with burgeoning audiences.

**Typical deal structure:**
- Free merch + Founding Member badge + custom referral link (no cash).
- Or: $50–200 per post for specifically-requested content.

**Target count:** Unlimited. 50–200 of these in 6 weeks if we have the sourcing bandwidth.

**Install volume per post:** 5–50. Individually trivial; cumulatively the backbone.

**CAC:** <$10 typically. The cheapest tier.

**Caveat:** Nano-influencer campaigns require high sourcing and coordination overhead. Quality of fit matters more than any other tier. Don't spray and pray.

**Scale up if:** Tier 3 hits sub-$10 CAC consistently. Double down there.
**Scale down if:** Any tier shows CAC >$50. Pause and investigate.

## 4. Sourcing playbook

### 4.1 Where to find candidates

- **Instagram search.** Hashtags: #hyroxtraining #naturalbodybuilding #powerliftingwomen #strengthcoach #hypertrophyprogramming. Scroll the top 100 posts; note accounts with 10k–100k followers, engagement >3%, and real training content.
- **YouTube niche channels.** Search "hyrox training program," "peaking for powerlifting meet," "hypertrophy training tips." Look for channels with 20k–300k subs.
- **Competitor teardown.** Who's promoting Hevy / Strong / RP / MacroFactor? Those accounts are already open to promoting fitness apps.
- **Gym networks.** Ask Sophia / existing ambassadors for recommendations from their networks.
- **Federation + event coverage.** USAPL, Hyrox, CrossFit regionals — scan who's posting from events.
- **Tools** (paid, optional): Upfluence, Modash, Heepsy — filter by audience demographic + geography + engagement. $100–500/mo. Worth it for Tier 2–3 sourcing volume.

### 4.2 Screening checklist

Before outreach:
- [ ] Audience fit (their followers are lifters, not general fitness).
- [ ] Engagement rate >3% (filter out bought followers).
- [ ] Recent posts (active, not dormant).
- [ ] Voice fit (not shouty, not bro-y, not body-shaming).
- [ ] No red flags (doping glorification, MLM involvement, gambling promotion, etc.).
- [ ] Prior sponsor history (if they've endorsed 5 apps this year, they're burnt. Skip.).

### 4.3 Outreach template

Subject: `Quick partnership question — @[their handle]`

> Hey [name],
>
> Caleb Suh here, founder of PHYZIK (phyzik.app). We're a new training app built for lifters — MEV/MAV/MRV programming, real social accountability, Hyrox coverage, hypertrophy depth. Launching this month.
>
> Your [specific content — e.g., "deadlift technique thread," "Hyrox sled push breakdown," "volume progression video"] is exactly the kind of training-serious content our audience would connect with.
>
> Two options to discuss:
> 1. **Ambassador track** (lower pressure, longer horizon — free merch + early access in exchange for being a real user and posting naturally when you want to).
> 2. **Paid partnership** (1–2 posts + Stories in exchange for a standard fee, TBD based on your rates).
>
> If either interests you, I'd love to hop on 20 minutes to show you the app and see what fits. If not, no worries — follow-up welcome anytime.
>
> — Caleb
> [optional: phyzik.app link, one screenshot, not a deck]

Rules:
- Specific content reference. Always.
- Offer two paths. Let them self-select.
- No deck attachment. A link + one screenshot.
- Don't mention budget in the first message.
- If they don't respond in 10 days, one follow-up. After that, move on.

## 5. Deal structure — what we pay for

### 5.1 Required deliverables (per Tier)

**Tier 2:**
- 1 feed post (static or Reel) with agreed timing.
- 3 Stories (at least 2 with the swipe-up/link sticker).
- Optional: 1 YouTube integration.
- Usage rights: we can use the content in our own marketing for 6 months.
- Custom referral code: `phyzik.app/i/{theirname}` featured in their caption.

**Tier 3:**
- 1 feed post OR 3 Stories (their choice of format).
- Referral code in bio for 30 days.
- Their creative. Our feedback rights on anything that misrepresents the product.

**Tier 4:**
- 1 post of any format featuring the app.
- Referral code for 30 days.

### 5.2 What we provide

- Brief (1-page, not 40-slide). Core brand voice points, the 3 things we'd love them to mention if natural, the things to avoid.
- 10–20 screenshots + b-roll + logo pack.
- Optional: pre-cut vertical video assets (workout share cards in motion) for easy Reel inclusion.
- Their custom referral link and tracking setup.

### 5.3 What we never require

- Specific caption copy. (Kills authenticity.)
- A specific number of hashtags. (Cringe.)
- Negative comparisons to competitors. (Tacky + legal risk.)
- "PHYZIK is the best app ever" language. (Destroys trust.)
- Posting within a specific hour window. (Unless a coordinated campaign.)

## 6. Campaign types

### 6.1 The "seed" campaign (weeks 1–2)

Goal: 5–10 Tier 3 activations to test conversion and iterate on briefs.

- Budget: $3k.
- KPI: Measure CAC per influencer. Identify best-fit niches.
- Deliverables: 1 post each, referral code, Stories.

### 6.2 The "niche wave" (weeks 3–4)

Goal: Go hard in the 2 best-performing niches from Seed. E.g., if Hyrox and hypertrophy-coaching both converted at <$15 CAC, double down.

- Budget: $15k across Tier 2 + Tier 3.
- KPI: Drive 2k installs at <$20 CAC.
- Deliverables: Stacked activations in same week (so we capture concentrated reach momentum).

### 6.3 The "anchor" (week 5)

Goal: 1–2 high-quality Tier 2 activations with strong content we can repurpose as paid-ad creative.

- Budget: $10k.
- KPI: Content quality + usage rights. Install volume is bonus.

### 6.4 The "long tail" (continuous, weeks 1–6)

Goal: Tier 4 activations via merch + referral, no cash. Low-volume, high-volume-aggregate.

- Budget: $3k (merch only).
- KPI: 50+ nano activations. Install volume 500+.

## 7. Measurement

### 7.1 Per-campaign dashboard

For every campaign:
- Total spend.
- Installs attributed to their code.
- First-workout completions (activated installs).
- Week-4 retention of that cohort.
- CAC (spend / activated installs).
- LTV proxy (sessions per user over 4 weeks — same cohort).

### 7.2 What good looks like

- Tier 2: <$30 CAC. <$60 is acceptable for the first campaign.
- Tier 3: <$15 CAC. <$30 is acceptable initially.
- Tier 4: <$10 CAC. <$20 is acceptable.
- Activation rate (installs → first workout): >40% across all tiers, or the targeting/brief is wrong.
- Retention at week 4: within 20% of our organic cohort. If way worse, the audience isn't a fit.

### 7.3 What kills a campaign

- CAC >$100: stop. Investigate.
- Activation <20%: the audience isn't training-oriented. Re-target.
- Retention <50% of organic: the brief is mismatched. Fix the brief.

## 8. Legal / compliance

- **FTC disclosure** is non-negotiable. Tier 2–4 influencers receiving any value must tag `#ad` or `#partnership` on paid posts per FTC 16 CFR Part 255. Include in every brief. Refusing hurts us more than them.
- **Platform policies.** IG, TikTok, YouTube all have their own paid-partnership disclosure tools (Paid Partnership tag). Require usage.
- **Contract terms per campaign.** A 1-page deal memo per Tier 2 deal. Tier 3+ can be email-based agreements ("Deliverables: 1 post + 3 Stories by [date]. Payment: $X on delivery. Usage rights: 6 months. Cancellation: up to 48h before posting, pro-rata.").
- **Medical / wellness claims.** Influencers must not make unsubstantiated claims about PHYZIK ("PHYZIK cured my back pain"). Brief addresses this.
- **IP — music, b-roll.** If we provide assets, we warrant they're cleared. If they use our assets and sync to Taylor Swift, that's on them; brief addresses.
- **Cross-reference `legal-compliance.md`** for the full picture.

## 9. Content we hand off to influencers

Package up a standard kit:

**Brand kit:**
- PHYZIK wordmark + logo variants.
- Accent color (#A78BFA) + typography notes.
- Voice do's and don'ts (1-pager).
- Do not morph the logo. Do not over-saturate the accent color. Do not use "shredded" or "shredz" language.

**Content kit:**
- 15 App Store screenshots.
- 10 b-roll clips of the app in motion (session logger, share card, Floor) — 1080x1920 + 1080x1080.
- 3 pre-cut hype Reels we've made (optional: they can use, remix, or ignore).
- Example captions (not prescribed — shown as "what others have said naturally").

Stored somewhere accessible: Dropbox link, Google Drive, or Notion. Not Figma — too designery.

## 10. Risks

1. **Paying for dud campaigns.** Mitigate: strict CAC ceiling ($100 Tier 2, $50 Tier 3). Kill fast.
2. **Influencer posts go south.** Off-brand captions, bad timing, body-shaming in comments. Mitigate: pre-approve captions optional (but encouraged for Tier 2). Monitor comments for 24h; respond if problematic.
3. **Exclusive deal poaching.** A competitor outbids us for an influencer we'd locked in. Mitigate: get the post before paying 100%. Staged payments (25% at brief acceptance, 75% at post live).
4. **Fake followers / engagement.** 30% of nano-micro-influencer accounts have purchased growth. Mitigate: use a tool (HypeAuditor free tier, Modash) or do a manual engagement-rate check before paying.
5. **Legal — unpaid endorsement claims.** If we gift merch without requiring a post, they don't owe us anything and don't need to disclose. If we require, they do. Mitigate: clarity on obligations in every deal.

---

## 11. Ethics — lines we don't cross

1. **No child influencers under 16.** Legal + ethical risk.
2. **No accounts whose primary value prop is body modification endorsement** (SARMs, anabolics, unregulated peptides). Huge legal risk for us in the U.S., and brand-misaligned.
3. **No weight-loss-transformation-pic content for PHYZIK.** We're a strength app. Transformation pics belong to MyFitnessPal / Noom. Our content is progression, not before/after.
4. **No "only works if you buy Pro" posts.** Pro isn't the point.
5. **No shaming competitor apps.** Strava, Hevy, Strong — all decent products. We don't need to punch down. Positive differentiation is stronger.
