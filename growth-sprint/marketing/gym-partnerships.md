# Marketing — Gym Partnerships

**Owner:** Caleb (relationships), Sophia + Ten Takeda (local gym credibility).
**Budget estimate:** $0 initially (relationship-led); $1–5k/month if we scale to paid placements.
**Grounded in:** `research/competitors.md` §Zwift §TrainHeroic §Strong, `research/growth-case-studies.md` §Whoop §Peloton.

Gym partnerships are **the slowest, most moat-building channel** in the stack. They take months to develop but the users they deliver retain at ~2–3× the organic rate (per industry benchmarks in similar apps). They also give us a local physical presence that no app-only competitor has.

At 6 weeks out from 50k, gym partnerships will **not** be the lever that hits that number. But they are the lever that makes month 4 and month 8 easier. The sprint goal is to seed 5–10 partnerships, not to scale them.

---

## 1. The strategic frame

### 1.1 Why gyms, specifically

A PHYZIK user at a gym that's "PHYZIK-aware" has:
- **Social proof in the room.** Other lifters are on the app. It's not a weird personal quirk; it's the gym's app.
- **Coaching continuity.** The coach uses PHYZIK. The lifter's app follows the coach's programming.
- **Return trigger.** Walking into the gym is a PHYZIK touchpoint — a Floor post from a squadmate, a QR code on the wall, a visible use at the squat rack.
- **Real friend layer.** Two people at the same gym on PHYZIK will find each other.

This compounds per gym. One gym with 10 PHYZIK users is a weak effect; same gym with 50 PHYZIK users is a self-perpetuating network effect.

### 1.2 The three partnership models

**Model 1: Official gym partner.**
- The gym actively integrates PHYZIK into operations.
- Coaches use it. Members are onboarded at signup.
- Gym gets branded referral link, possibly a rev share (when Pro launches).
- We get volume + credibility.

**Model 2: Affiliate / ambassador gym.**
- Gym owner is personally a PHYZIK ambassador.
- Endorses informally. Posts occasionally. Refers high-value members.
- No operational integration, just personal advocacy.

**Model 3: Community partner.**
- Gym hosts PHYZIK events (meetups, filming shoots, demo days).
- Permission-level relationship. Gym isn't a PHYZIK channel, but the relationship allows in-person presence.

**Our 6-week target:** 3 Model 1, 5–8 Model 2, 3–5 Model 3.

## 2. The ideal partner profile

Not every gym is a fit. Use this filter:

**Qualify:**
- [ ] Caters to serious lifters (powerlifting-equipped, proper bars, real plates, chalk allowed).
- [ ] Has an active coaching program (so coaches can prescribe PHYZIK).
- [ ] Owner or head coach has an audience (IG, YouTube, local reputation).
- [ ] Alignment with our voice (no "transformation" culture, no body-shaming, no bro-science).
- [ ] Located in a growth market (NYC, LA, Austin, London, Sydney, Berlin, Stockholm, Miami).

**Disqualify:**
- [ ] Planet Fitness-style chains. Wrong audience.
- [ ] "Transformation" boutique studios. Brand mismatch.
- [ ] Franchise gyms where the owner can't make decisions (wasted time).
- [ ] Gym owners with reputations for doping evangelism or bro-science (legal + brand risk).
- [ ] Chain Orangetheory, F45, Barry's. Their programming is proprietary; PHYZIK doesn't fit their model.

### 2.1 Gym archetypes to target

**Archetype A: The strength training temple.**
- Westside Barbell adjacents.
- Kabuki Strength-affiliated gyms.
- Any gym run by a USAPL / IPF coach with an audience.
- Fit: very high. Customer base is our direct target.

**Archetype B: The Hyrox / hybrid gym.**
- Hyrox affiliate gyms.
- Any gym running structured Hyrox programming.
- Fit: very high. Ten's angle unlocks this.

**Archetype C: The high-end independent performance gym.**
- Cressey Sports Performance (MA), EXOS (various), Landow Performance (CO).
- Performance-oriented rather than aesthetic-oriented.
- Fit: high. Coaches here need programming tools; their clients are premium.

**Archetype D: The independent CrossFit affiliate.**
- CrossFit boxes with a good strength culture.
- Fit: medium-high. Some alignment with Hyrox athletes.

**Archetype E: The bodybuilding / physique gym.**
- Prep-focused gyms.
- Typically 1 strong coach + 5–15 clients.
- Fit: high. Sophia's angle.

## 4. The pitch

### 4.1 First touch

DM to gym owner (IG is usually best; email works if public):

> Hi [name], Caleb here — I'm building PHYZIK, a training app for lifters (phyzik.app). I've been researching [city]'s strength scene and [gym name] keeps coming up as a serious place.
>
> Not selling you anything. I'd like to come train for a session (or video-visit) and, if it resonates, talk about a simple partnership: your members get accelerated access to PHYZIK, we feature [gym] as a founding partner gym. No cost to you.
>
> Coffee or a training slot this month? I can fit around your schedule.

Rules:
- Specific: reference their gym specifically.
- Low-friction ask: "training session + conversation" beats "call."
- Explicit frame: "not selling you anything" calms the reflex.
- Free: partnership must be free for them. Charge later, never upfront.

### 4.2 The visit / call

Goal: 60–90 minutes. Half trainer-to-trainer, half business.

Structure:
1. **Train first (or watch them coach for 30 minutes).** Builds trust. Shows we're lifters, not salespeople.
2. **Tour / facility conversation.** Compliment honestly. Notice their systems.
3. **Sit down, show app on phone.** Walk through the Floor, the session logger, the programming. Let them play.
4. **Pitch the partnership.** See §5.
5. **Ask: what would make this useful to you?** Listen more than you talk.
6. **Leave a clear next step.** "I'll send a follow-up with the partnership structure. If you're in, we can have you live within 2 weeks."

## 5. Partnership mechanics (by model)

### 5.1 Model 1 — Official gym partner

**What the gym does:**
- Onboards new members by showing PHYZIK at signup.
- Head coach (or whoever prescribes programs) uses PHYZIK for all programming.
- Hangs a framed "PHYZIK partner gym" certificate in the gym (we provide).
- Includes PHYZIK in welcome email + onboarding doc.
- Posts 1–2× a month on IG featuring PHYZIK during training.
- Provides testimonial / photo we can feature on `/community`.

**What we provide:**
- Custom referral link: `phyzik.app/gym/{slug}`.
- All new members via that link get: first 3 months of Pro (when Pro ships), Founding Member badge, waived onboarding friction.
- Gym owner + head coach get: Tier B ambassador status + merch + 1-year Pro.
- Framed in-gym signage.
- Featured on `/community` gym partner section.
- Co-branded content: we post their gym on @phyzikapp occasionally; they post us.
- Rev share on Pro subscriptions from their gym link: 20% for 12 months (when Pro ships). Honest economics, not exploitation.
- Priority feature request path (we can't promise features, but their voice lands in our backlog with weight).

**What we do NOT provide:**
- Cash upfront.
- Exclusivity on other apps (they can keep Hevy for members who prefer, etc.).
- Any discount on something we sell to them (we're not selling them anything; that's the whole point).

**Deal structure:**
- 1-page Memorandum of Understanding. Not a legal contract — a clear terms-sheet.
- 6-month initial term. Auto-continues unless either side wants to end.
- Mutual 30-day off-ramp.

### 5.2 Model 2 — Ambassador gym

**What the gym does:**
- Owner becomes a Tier B ambassador (per `ambassador-program.md`).
- Post occasionally featuring PHYZIK — natural, their voice.
- Refer their best members informally.

**What we provide:**
- Tier B ambassador benefits (merch, badge, early access).
- Custom gym referral link.
- Recognition on `/community`.

**Deal structure:**
- Email agreement. Super lightweight.

### 5.3 Model 3 — Community partner

**What the gym does:**
- Allows us to host a PHYZIK event (meetup, filming, demo day) at their gym.
- Allows their members to attend.
- Potentially co-promotes the event.

**What we provide:**
- Event costs (modest: catering, merch to give out).
- Exposure to PHYZIK's channels.
- Long-term relationship option if it works out.

**Deal structure:**
- Event-by-event handshake agreement.

## 6. In-gym execution (Model 1 specifically)

### 6.1 Onboarding new members

When a gym signs up a new member, they hand them a card (physical) with:
- QR code to `phyzik.app/gym/{slug}`.
- One-liner: "Your programming lives in PHYZIK. Scan to download."

The landing page:
- Gym logo + photo.
- "Partner of [Gym Name]" signal.
- "Free for [Gym Name] members. Pro unlocked for 3 months."
- Install CTA.

### 6.2 Coach-prescribed programming

The gym's head coach uses PHYZIK's program builder (when ready) to create the gym's core programs. Members at the gym can pick "[Gym]'s Hypertrophy Block" or "[Gym]'s Hyrox Prep" from their program list.

This requires:
- Shareable programs (per `missing-features.md` §3.1). Currently unbuilt; weeks 3–4.
- Coach attribution on programs.

**Until the program builder ships:** Model 1 partnerships are soft. The coach uses PHYZIK for logging but has to hand-write programming to members off-platform. Make this explicit in the pitch.

### 6.3 In-gym signage

A framed 8x10 "PHYZIK Partner Gym" certificate. Design it to be obviously tasteful — not a kitsch marketing poster. The gym hangs it at the front desk or above the squat rack.

Optionally: a small window sticker (think "We accept Apple Pay" aesthetic, not "Drink Coca-Cola").

Cost: ~$25 per gym. Shipping included. Minimal.

### 6.4 Social / content

Monthly rhythm:
- We feature one partner gym on @phyzikapp's feed (1 post, 2–3 Stories).
- They post a PHYZIK-adjacent piece (training at gym, new program, member highlight).
- Occasional reciprocal stories.

Don't force cadence. Healthy relationships have natural rhythm.

## 7. Events — the lightweight catalyst

Real, in-person events are disproportionately powerful at this stage.

**Event ideas:**

### 7.1 Open gym PHYZIK meetup

- 2-hour open session at a partner gym.
- Existing users come train together.
- Caleb + Sophia show up, hang out, no pitch deck.
- Free merch, free coffee.
- Content: shot as a Reel, posted by the gym + PHYZIK.

Cost: $500–1000 (merch + coffee). Value: high. Every attendee becomes a public PHYZIK user + the content compounds.

### 7.2 Programming workshop

- Sophia runs a 90-minute workshop at a partner gym on volume landmarks or mesocycle design.
- PHYZIK's approach is used as the worked example.
- Free for gym members; charged modestly for non-members to convert.

Cost: $0 if Sophia donates time; gym provides space. Low-effort, high-trust.

### 7.3 Hyrox prep camp

- Ten runs a 4-hour Hyrox prep session.
- Uses PHYZIK as the programming backbone for the session.
- Charged event ($75–150/person), revenue split with gym.
- Content gold: video, photos, testimonials.

Cost: Time, some travel. Revenue-positive.

### 7.4 Athlete shoot days

- Ten / Sophia / other athletes film training content at a partner gym for a day.
- Gym gets brand placement in the content.
- Content goes to IG, marketing site, App Store screenshots.

Cost: Production + athlete time. Value: content asset + gym relationship deepened.

## 8. Rev share economics (when Pro ships)

For Model 1 partners:
- 20% of Pro revenue from members via their link, for 12 months from their install.
- Example math: Pro = $9.99/mo. A gym delivers 100 Pro subs. Their share: $9.99 × 0.20 × 100 = $200/mo. For a year. $2400/yr. Per 100 subscribers.

Is that a lot? No. But it's not nothing, and it's aligned. A gym that drives 500 paid subs gets $12k/yr with zero additional work after setup.

**Alternative structures we might consider:**
- Flat monthly referral fee ($100/mo per gym) instead of rev share. Simpler accounting; good for small gyms.
- Equity in free app (badges, early access) only. No money. For gyms where money feels awkward.

**Rule:** Never structure a deal that extracts from the gym. The deal must be additive for them. If the gym loses time or money or attention relative to not partnering, the deal will die within 6 months.

## 9. Measurement

Per partner gym:
- Installs attributed to their link (weekly).
- Member retention (4-week, 8-week).
- Member Pro conversion rate (when Pro).
- Referral activity of their members.

Overall program:
- Active partner gyms.
- Average installs per gym per month.
- Partner gym retention (how many are we still partnered with at month 6, month 12?).
- Revenue from rev share (when Pro).

Warning signals:
- A partner gym with zero installs in 30 days: the setup wasn't implemented. Fix friction or unwind.
- A partner gym churning members (members cancelling both the gym and PHYZIK): investigate independently.

## 10. Risks

1. **Partner gym reputation liability.** A gym owner makes a public misstep (doping promo, sexist comment, whatever). We're associated. Mitigate: careful vetting, 30-day off-ramp clause, monitor partner content loosely.
2. **Coach drops out.** The head coach leaves the gym and the programming backbone collapses. Mitigate: relationship with 2+ people at each gym (owner + head coach at minimum).
3. **Gym closes.** Small gym economics are brutal. Mitigate: maintain several partners per city; don't bet a city on one gym.
4. **Exclusivity requests.** A gym asks to be "the exclusive PHYZIK gym in [city]." Mitigate: decline exclusivity except in very small towns. Multiple partnerships per city are healthier.
5. **Unused partnership.** A gym signs the MOU then does nothing. Mitigate: mutual 30-day off-ramp. Don't be afraid to sunset dead partnerships.
6. **Legal / commercial:** MOUs are simple but should still exist in writing. Rev-share requires accurate attribution. Mitigate: get the link-attribution pipeline built BEFORE signing rev-share deals.

## 15. Anti-patterns

1. **Signing too many gyms too fast.** 10 Model 1s in 6 weeks is impossible to support. 3 is reasonable.
2. **Paying gym owners cash upfront.** Sets a bad precedent. Once you've paid, they expect pay. Equity + rev share + merch are healthier.
3. **Demanding exclusivity.** Keeps the gym from signing with us.
4. **Letting partnerships go dark.** A partner gym unused for 60 days is dead weight. Audit quarterly; sunset ruthlessly.
5. **Scaling before we've seen one gym work well.** Prove the model at 1 gym (Ten's or Sophia's). Then copy the playbook.
6. **Under-investing in the framed certificate's design.** It's the most visible artifact of the partnership — it should look like something the gym is proud to display, not a laminated ad.
