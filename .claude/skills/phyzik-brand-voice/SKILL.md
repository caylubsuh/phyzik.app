# PHYZIK Brand Voice and Positioning

## What PHYZIK is
A social training platform built for lifters — the community layer the weight room has been waiting for.

## Who PHYZIK is for
Anyone in the gym, training with intent. Ultra-marathon runners, competitive bodybuilders, high schoolers, working professionals, total beginners. If they lift, they are in.

## Primary taglines (use verbatim when possible)
- "The social training platform built for lifters."
- "The social training platform the weight room has been waiting for."
- "Every lifter. Every goal. One platform."

## Voice
- Direct and confident. No hedging, no "we are exploring," no "probably," no "we plan to."
- Specific over abstract. Real exercises, real numbers, real scenarios beat generalities.
- Mission-led without being preachy. Show the inclusivity through action, not DEI list-checking.
- Conversational-professional. Closer to Patrick Collison (Stripe) than corporate fitness marketing.
- Blunt when needed. Profanity is fine in internal docs, rare in consumer copy.
- Short sentences. Em-dashes earn their place. Strategic line breaks for rhythm.

## What we always say
- "Lifters," "people in the gym," "training with intent"
- "The Floor" (always capitalized — our social feed)
- "Squads" (our group competition feature)
- "Progressive overload," "volume landmarks," "deload," "mesocycle"
- Specific research citations where earned: Schoenfeld, Israetel, Baz-Valle, Pritchard, Ogasawara
- "Free" — confidently, without conditions or timelines

## What we NEVER say
- "Serious lifters" — excludes the people we are for
- "First 500 users" — we are free, not limited-free
- "Always free" or "free forever" — do not commit to pricing in perpetuity
- "Gym bros," "bro science" — we are science-forward, not jokey
- Competitor names (Hevy, Strong, Strava, Fitbod, MyFitnessPal, MacroFactor, etc.) — we do not negotiate attention against competitors
- "We are still exploring" / "may introduce" / "planning to" / "eventually" — no roadmap hedging on consumer surfaces
- Generic SaaS filler: "empower," "unlock potential," "game-changer," "revolutionary," "cutting-edge," "seamless," "holistic"
- "Workout logging app" — reductive; we are a platform
- "Transform your body" or before-and-after framing — we are about the process, not the diet-industry outcome

## Positioning against competitors (without naming them)
When differentiating, frame as structural choice, not as "we are better than X":
- "Most lifting apps treat tracking and social as separate products. PHYZIK merges them."
- "Other platforms segment you by who you are. PHYZIK is built around what you do."
- "The usual lifting-app pattern is an empty template you fill in yourself. PHYZIK writes the program."

## Inclusivity framing
Led by action, not identity. When listing demographic categories (as in the FAQ Q1 answer), frame as "we do not care about any of these things; we care that you are here" — active and dismissive of the barriers, never a compliance checklist.

## Science-forward claims
Any claim about programming, hypertrophy, or methodology must be defensible against peer-reviewed literature. Approved reference bodies:
- Schoenfeld (volume, frequency, proximity to failure)
- Israetel and Renaissance Periodization (MEV/MAV/MRV volume landmark theory, mesocycle design)
- Baz-Valle (training to failure meta-analyses)
- Pritchard (exercise selection for hypertrophy)
- Ogasawara (mechanical tension)
- Helms (natural bodybuilding methodology, RIR-based prescription)
Never invent citations. If unsure of the specific source, describe the principle without citing.

## Pricing posture
PHYZIK is completely free. Full stop. Do NOT elaborate future monetization plans on consumer surfaces. If pressed in FAQ-style contexts, answer "yes, free, no asterisk" with mission conviction. Revenue exploration (B2B gym partnerships, optional advanced tiers, advertising) is an INTERNAL topic — never website copy.

## Copy-review checklist
Apply this before any copy ships:
1. Does it hedge? Remove hedging.
2. Does it name a competitor? Remove the name.
3. Does it commit to future pricing or roadmap timing? Remove the commitment.
4. Is it specific or generic? Rewrite for specificity.
5. Does it sound like PHYZIK or like any generic SaaS company? If generic, rewrite.
6. Would Caleb say this on a podcast without flinching? If not, rewrite.
7. Does it treat lifters with respect, or does it condescend? Respect only.

## Tone examples — good vs bad

BAD: "Unlock your potential with our game-changing AI-powered training platform."
GOOD: "The split adapts to your numbers. Every session."

BAD: "PHYZIK is the ultimate app for serious lifters who want to take their training to the next level."
GOOD: "Built for anyone in the gym, training with intent."

BAD: "We are committed to always keeping the core experience free for all users."
GOOD: "Free. No trial, no asterisk."

BAD: "Unlike Hevy or Strong, PHYZIK includes social features."
GOOD: "Most lifting apps treat tracking and social as separate products. PHYZIK merges them."
